Quantitative LCA: Polypropylene vs. Steel Hair Clip

Building a hands-on comparative Life Cycle Assessment model from scratch to calculate embodied carbon, identify supply chain hotspots, and prove how durability assumptions can invert sustainable material claims.

01

Methodology: The "Learning-by-Making" Approach

Designers frequently make sweeping claims about "eco-friendly" materials—presuming that natural or metallic materials are inherently superior to polymers. Rather than relying on passive reading or marketing claims, this self-directed exercise sought to master Life Cycle Assessment (LCA) methodology through direct, rigorous calculation.

Defining the Functional Unit & System Boundaries

In ISO 14040/44 frameworks, valid comparisons require an identical functional performance metric. For this inquiry, the functional unit was defined as:

Standardized Functional Unit

"Holding an average volume of human hair securely during daily waking hours over exactly one year of regular domestic use."

System boundaries were defined as cradle-to-grave: raw polymer/ore extraction, refining, molding/stamping, transport, and municipal solid waste incineration/landfill.

03

Sensitivity Analysis & The Critical Pivot

Methodological Realization & Pivot

Why Static Claims are Misleading: Early drafts assumed the 75% carbon advantage of metal was an absolute truth. However, executing sensitivity analysis proved how fragile this conclusion was. If a user misplaces their metal hair clip within 4 months (a common occurrence with small personal accessories), or if the metal clip requires decorative chromium plating and thick wire gauges, the metal clip’s environmental footprint surpasses that of the plastic clip. The project pivoted from making an absolute scientific claim to demonstrating deep mastery over sensitivity testing and boundary modeling.

"LCAs are highly sensitive to boundary definitions and material durability assumptions; building an LCA model manually reveals where environmental hotspots actually lie." — Quantitative Analysis Learning

Application to Physical Hardware

Building this quantitative model equips a product manager with the ability to cut through greenwashing. The exact same calculation framework can be deployed to evaluate material choices and durability trade-offs in complex hardware products like V. POCKET or the Focus Dial.
