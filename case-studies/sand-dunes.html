Sand Dunes: Material & Light Study

Abstract cast cement forms capturing the temporal movement of natural wind erosion through the dynamic interaction of directional raking light, cast shadows, and negative space.

Role Individual Maker
Medium & Tools Cast Cement, Mold Making, Directional Raking Light Sources
Design Philosophies Wabi-Sabi (Imperfection), Ma (Negative Space), Kanso (Simplicity), Shizen (Naturalness)
01

Philosophical Grounding: Wabi-Sabi & Ma

Modern digital fabrication often chases mathematical perfection and frictionless surfaces. In contrast, Sand Dunes was conceived as a tactile inquiry into traditional Japanese aesthetics:

  • Wabi-Sabi: Finding profound beauty in impermanence, asymmetry, and raw material roughness.
  • Ma (間): The conscious celebration of the negative space, interval, and pause that gives definition to solid matter.
  • Kanso & Shizen: Eliminating ornamental clutter to allow natural material behavior to speak directly.
"Capturing the feeling of natural movement requires minimal form and celebrated imperfection rather than realistic landscape replication." — Material Exploration Reflection
02

Casting Technique & The Minimalist Pivot

Form Simplification Pivot

Why Literal Landscapes Failed: Initial clay sketches attempted to literally reproduce the ripples of a desert dune. When cast in cement, however, realistic ripples looked kitsch and visually noisy. The design pivoted decisively toward abstract minimal undulating planes—allowing gentle gradients of shadow to convey wind and fluid movement without literal imitation.

The physical model was hand-sculpted in negative molds and cast using Portland cement with varied aggregate fines. The resulting piece features subtle surface variations: air pocket voids, coarse grit pockets, and knife-sharp ridge crests.

03

Limitations & Architectural Applications

Documented Information Gaps

This project is an experimental material study. Quantitative measurements and digital CAD models were intentionally omitted in favor of manual haptic intuition.

Architectural Scale-Up

While built as a desktop study specimen, the tactile language points toward larger architectural applications: modular cast acoustic wall panels that diffuse sound through geometric contouring, or interior surfaces that subtly change mood as daylight travels across a room.
